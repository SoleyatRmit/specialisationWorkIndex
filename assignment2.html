Parallel Prototypes: Dilation

Dilation is an instrument that works differently from a traditional timer. It focuses entirely on relaxation, not on watching the timer. During a session there are no numbers, so you don't know how many minutes or seconds are left, and that's on purpose. The full concept is on my Assignment 1 page.

Layout

Layout covers the setup screen, where you choose everything before a session starts. I want feedback on which layout gives the better experience, and which one is easiest to navigate.

Prototype 1: Stacked

A stacked layout, with everything on one screen. The settings are grouped into Time and Sound, and it all fits on a laptop screen without scrolling. It suits Dilation because it's simple. Everything is grouped and easy to understand, and you see every setting as soon as you open the page.

Prototype 3: Pad

The idea came from Ableton's Learning Synths (Ableton n.d.), which we were shown in class. It has a pad that uses spatial input to change the sound. Here, left to right sets the pitch, and up and down sets the brightness. The pad also gives you a preview: while you drag, you hear what the session is going to sound like before you begin. That's the purpose from my Assignment 1, deciding what the time sounds like before it starts. It also explores spatial input, another of the extended techniques.

Prototype 5: Closing-in chimes

A bit more complicated. Instead of one bell, the chimes are part of the session. The first rings at halfway, then each one rings halfway through whatever time is left, so they get closer together until they're about a second apart. Then a lower final chime lands right at the end. Because there are several chimes, it feels like part of the session instead of one bell to end it.

Prototype 6: Countdown

Simple: the fade out is counted down in numbers at the end of the session. If you're not looking at the screen you'd miss it, so I added a tick with each number. The bigger problem is that it has numbers. My whole goal was no numbers and no sense of the exact time, and this goes against that. That's exactly why I want peer feedback on it, because nothing tells you a session is ending more clearly than a countdown.
